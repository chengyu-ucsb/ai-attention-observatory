# Design directions and research-to-product roadmap

### Public website: Editorial Observatory + Evidence Atlas

Use a crimson editorial identity, purposeful whitespace, strong typography, an original attention-network SVG, and real charts/tables derived from index JSON. Weekly highlights must be selected for substantive relevance and source-supported findings; an automatic summary is not an independent evidence appraisal. Do not claim counts are an overall literature census.

### The Experimental Lab (early ideas only)

**Focus Sprint.** A short, voluntary session centered on an explicit intention. Candidate outcome measures: interruptions per session, adherence to self-chosen task, perceived autonomy, workload, and sustained-attention performance.

**Think before delegating.** Let people request different levels of help (reflection, hint, structured outline) before seeing a complete AI response. A future study could compare accuracy, calibration, delayed recall, independent reasoning and frustration across assistance conditions.

**Return Point.** Capture the next concrete action before an interruption; display it at the moment of resuming work. Candidate outcomes include resumption lag, task errors and cognitive load, with a comparison against no checkpoint.

These prototypes are not therapeutic interventions or validated behavior-change tools. The current versions are client-side demonstrations with no data collection or AI API connection. User-entered text is held only in in-page JavaScript memory until the page reloads.

### Research-to-product approach

Track: proposed mechanisms → target setting/population → measurable outcomes → adverse effects or autonomy tradeoffs → testable experiment → results. Avoid generalizing from digital multitasking research to AI-specific causal effects without direct evidence. Avoid friction features that become additional distractions.

### Future iterations

- Literature synthesis reviewed by humans, source-cited statistical figures and meaningful paper-to-paper comparisons.
- Accessible mobile experience and usability testing.
- A secure, opt-in newsletter provider (the current site offers RSS and a mailto interest link only).
- A modest back-end for saved user sessions only after defining consent, retention and privacy protections.
