November lab updateUpdated 6 Nov 2026 · What changed?

Return Point now offers paired comparisons with parallel fictional tasks, randomized condition and task order, and shuffled answers. Design precedents now use mechanism sketches and expandable access/source notes, keeping the key limits visible without hiding the detail.

01 / A SHORT COGNITIVE CHALLENGE

The Switch Lab

Respond to a number. At first the rule stays the same; then the cue starts changing. Does switching feel different?

ODD / EVEN1, 3, 7 → LEFT
2, 4, 8 → RIGHT
LOW / HIGH1–4 → LEFT
6–9 → RIGHT

Use the buttons or ← / → arrow keys within the number panel. The snapshot has 20 trials with no deadline. Untimed mode lets you read feedback and advance at your own pace; it is useful with spoken cues. Leaving an activity excludes that response from timing.

Choose before starting. Practice is always untimed and never enters the snapshot.

02 / AN INTERRUPTION MINI-GAME

Return Point

Remember the next action in a small research task. Complete a distraction, then try to pick up where you left off.

Single-round mode

Single rounds let you explore freely. For a structured comparison, try one round in each condition with parallel fictional tasks.

Starts a fresh pair. Unfinished play is replaced; earlier single-round history stays. Starting condition and task order are randomized.

Leaving discards this comparison only. Your earlier single rounds and selected mode stay.

03 / AN AI-ASSISTANCE EXPERIMENT

Assistance Dial

Same reasoning problem, three ways an assistant might respond. Choose a level of help, then make the call yourself.

Fictional scenarios and scripted assistance, not a connected AI model. It's a design hypothesis about autonomy, not evidence that one level is best.

OUTSIDE THE LAB / DESIGN PRECEDENTS

How products approach focus.

Compare design mechanisms, without mistaking product claims for outcome evidence.

Official guides · Checked 6 Nov 2026Design examples, not independent outcome evidence or endorsements. Plans and availability can change.

  • 01 / DELIBERATE PAUSE

    one sec · Intention Tracking

    Try to open→Pause→Name intent

    After a breathing intervention, choose or type why you are opening an app.

    iPhone / iPad · Plan tier unlisted

    Intention Tracking guide
    Access, sources & trade-offs

    Access: Documented only for iPhone and iPad. The official plan comparison does not specify this feature’s tier; check in-app before choosing a plan.

    Design question: Does naming an intention change the next action, or add an extra tap?

    one sec plan comparison

  • 02 / ACCESS RESTRICTION

    Freedom · Blocking sessions

    Select→Block→Resume

    Block selected websites and apps during a session, with devices and duration chosen by you.

    Free sessions ≤2 hours · Scheduling paid

    Freedom setup guide
    Access, sources & trade-offs

    Access: Account and installation required; capabilities vary by platform. Free accounts allow immediate sessions up to two hours. Scheduling, longer sessions and Locked Mode require Premium.

    Design question: When does firm blocking help, and when are exceptions or a softer pause preferable?

    Freedom free and paid plans

  • 03 / SELECTIVE CONTENT

    Apple · Focus filters

    Focus→Filter→View

    Choose which information supported apps show during a Focus, such as a mail account or calendar. This is not a general app blocker.

    iPhone, iOS 16+ · App support varies

    Apple Focus setup guide
    Access, sources & trade-offs

    Access: Built into compatible iPhone software, with Focus filters introduced in iOS 16. App support varies, and Focus filters do not sync across devices.

    Design question: Could showing only relevant content preserve useful access without blocking an entire app?

    iOS 16 feature introduction

  • 04 / SYSTEM CONTROLS

    Android · Focus mode

    Select apps→Pause→Resume

    Pause selected apps and their notifications. Start manually, set a schedule, or take a timed break.

    Supported Android phones · Setup varies

    Android Digital Wellbeing guide
    Access, sources & trade-offs

    Access: Available on supported Android phones; settings vary by device. Digital Wellbeing asks for a profile during initial setup.

    Design question: What would a new attention tool add beyond an existing system pause and schedule?

ABOUT & METHODOLOGY / 04

Making attention legible.

A public reading room for the questions AI raises about thinking, focus, and human agency.

WHY THIS EXISTS

Less noise. Better questions. More substantive evidence.

AI is changing the conditions under which people work, learn, and direct attention. This observatory brings together empirical studies, conceptual scholarship, interventions, and strong public commentary while preserving the differences between them.

About the reading process

AI-assisted summaries and extracted passages are not independent peer review. Source details retain publication metadata, the material used, and its limits. The collection is selective, not a systematic review.
