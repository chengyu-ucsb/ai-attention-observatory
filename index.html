EXPERIMENTAL LAB / EARLY-STAGE CONCEPTS

Design for deliberate attention.

Research-inspired interaction sketches for a possible future app. Try the mechanics, think through the trade-offs, and help shape the next iteration.

The goal isn't another app that simply demands more productivity. These prototypes explore how interfaces might support self-directed focus, reflection, and recovery after interruptions. They are deliberately small and designed for exploration—not validated as interventions.

Prototype status: Experimental, browser-based demos. They do not monitor your real attention, access AI services, or submit text to a server. The exercises have not been tested for effectiveness.

Return Point

A lightweight checkpoint preserves where you were and your very next action, so you have somewhere to return after a distraction. Your text remains in this page's temporary state.

Write your checkpoint, then simulate a distraction to see how an attention re-entry cue could work.

PRODUCT HYPOTHESES / UNTESTED

Three ideas worth studying.

Voluntary structure: Would a lightweight focus intention reduce context switching without becoming another interruption? Graduated assistance: Could choosing hints before finished answers encourage active reasoning? Re-entry cues: Do captured next steps reduce the effort of resuming work after interruption? These are research questions, not demonstrated benefits.

ABOUT & METHODOLOGY / 04

Making attention legible.

A public reading room for a fast-moving research problem, not a replacement for critical evaluation of the original literature.

WHY THIS EXISTS

Less noise. Better questions. More substantive evidence.

AI is changing the conditions under which people work, learn, and direct attention. This observatory brings together empirical studies, conceptual scholarship, interventions, and strong public commentary while preserving the differences between them.

Explore the collection ↗
01

Discover

Search metadata from OpenAlex and Crossref and curated news RSS every week; record update dates and deduplicate results.

02

Read what is available

Retrieve abstracts and permitted open-access text when accessible. Article cards identify whether claims come from abstracts, full text, or citation-only metadata.

03

Show limits

Study excerpts are automatically selected rather than expert-reviewed. Lack of an abstract is not evidence of an absent method or result; diagrams illustrate concepts, not measured effects.

04

Keep updating

Scheduled workflows maintain the public bibliography and an RSS feed. Automation may be affected by source outages or rate limits, so the latest successful index date is displayed.
